Task 1 explanation

This exploratory data analysis (EDA) thoroughly examines the traffic accident dataset in Barcelona from 2019 to 2025, revealing critical temporal, spatial, and severity patterns while verifying dataset completeness and consistency.

Temporally, yearly accident counts dropped sharply in 2020 due to COVID-19 mobility restrictions, falling from over 10,000 cases in 2019 to around 6,200. Following the pandemic, accident volume experienced a partial rebound and stabilized between 7,500 and 8,000 cases annually. Throughout the day, accidents show a distinct bimodal peak during afternoon hours (12:00–18:00), which accounts for the highest incident frequency, followed by the evening commute (18:00–24:00). Conversely, early morning hours (00:00–06:00) record the lowest traffic incident volume.

Spatially, incidents are heavily concentrated in central commercial and high-density transit corridors. Eixample stands out as the primary hotspot with over 15,000 recorded accidents, substantially outnumbering other major urban districts such as Sant Martí and Sants-Montjuïc. Dense intersection networks and heavy vehicular throughput likely drive this pronounced spatial clustering.

Regarding severity, the overwhelming majority of recorded accidents (97.4%) involve minor injuries (Leve). Severe injuries (Grave) represent 2.4% of total cases, while fatal incidents (Mortal) remain extremely rare at 0.2%. Data quality checks confirmed minimal missing coordinates and coherent field distributions. These insights suggest that future visualization design should prioritize filtering by peak hours and high-risk districts like Eixample to support targeted municipal traffic management.
